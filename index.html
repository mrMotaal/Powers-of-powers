🌟 Idea 1: Multiplication and Division

Multiplication & Division Rules

Tap to reveal core secrets 👆

Key Formulas

  • Multiplication: \( a^{n} \times a^{m} = a^{n+m} \)
  • Example: \( x^{3} \times x^{4} = x^{7} \)
  • Division: \( a^{n} \div a^{m} = a^{n-m} \)
  • Example: \( \frac{x^{5}}{x^{3}} = x^{2} \)
Example 1.1
Simplify the following expression:
\( \frac{(2^{11})^{2} \times 2^{3}}{(2^{6})^{3} \times 2^{4}} \)
Apply power of a power rule in numerator and denominator:
Numerator: \( (2^{11})^{2} = 2^{22} \)
Denominator: \( (2^{6})^{3} = 2^{18} \)
Expression becomes: \( \frac{2^{22} \times 2^{3}}{2^{18} \times 2^{4}} \)
Apply multiplication rule (add exponents):
Numerator: \( 2^{22+3} = 2^{25} \)
Denominator: \( 2^{18+4} = 2^{22} \)
Expression becomes: \( \frac{2^{25}}{2^{22}} \)
Apply division rule (subtract exponents):
\( 2^{25-22} = 2^{3} \)
\( = 8 \)
Example 1.2
Simplify the following expression:
\( \frac{(X^{2})^{-3} \times (X^{-1})^{2}}{X^{-3} \times X^{-4}} \)
Apply power of a power rule in numerator:
\( (X^{2})^{-3} = X^{-6} \)
\( (X^{-1})^{2} = X^{-2} \)
Expression becomes: \( \frac{X^{-6} \times X^{-2}}{X^{-3} \times X^{-4}} \)
Apply multiplication rule (add exponents):
Numerator: \( X^{-6 + (-2)} = X^{-8} \)
Denominator: \( X^{-3 + (-4)} = X^{-7} \)
Expression becomes: \( \frac{X^{-8}}{X^{-7}} \)
Apply division rule (subtract exponents):
\( X^{-8 - (-7)} = X^{-8 + 7} = X^{-1} \)
\( = \frac{1}{X} \)
✍️ Try It Yourself 1
Simplify the following expression:
\( (\sqrt{5})^{3} \times ((-\sqrt{5})^{2})^{3} \)
Simplify the second term:
\( (-\sqrt{5})^{2} = 5 \)
So, \( ((-\sqrt{5})^{2})^{3} = 5^{3} \)
Now look at the first term:
\( (\sqrt{5})^{3} = \sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5} \times \sqrt{5} = 5\sqrt{5} \)
Multiply both parts:
\( 5\sqrt{5} \times 5^{3} = 5\sqrt{5} \times 125 \)
\( = 625\sqrt{5} \)

🌟 Idea 2: Distribution and Power of a Power

Distribution & Power Rules

Tap to reveal core secrets 👆

Key Formulas

  • Dist. on x: \( (a \times b)^{n} = a^{n} \times b^{n} \)
  • Power of a Power: \( (a^{n})^{m} = a^{n \times m} \)
  • Dist. on ÷: \( (\frac{a}{b})^{n} = \frac{a^{n}}{b^{n}} \)
  • Negative Power: \( (\frac{a}{b})^{-n} = (\frac{b}{a})^{n} \)
Example 2.1
Simplify the following expression:
\( (-2x^{2}y)^{3} \times (x^{-1}y^{2})^{-1} \)
Distribute powers to each term:
First part: \( (-2)^{3}(x^{2})^{3}(y)^{3} = -8x^{6}y^{3} \)
Second part: \( (x^{-1})^{-1}(y^{2})^{-1} = x^{1}y^{-2} \)
Multiply the simplified parts:
\( -8x^{6}y^{3} \times xy^{-2} \)
Combine like terms (add exponents):
\( -8(x^{6} \times x^{1})(y^{3} \times y^{-2}) \)
\( -8(x^{6+1})(y^{3-2}) \)
\( = -8x^{7}y \)
Example 2.2
Simplify the following expression:
\( (\frac{2a^{2}b^{2}}{b^{-1}a^{4}})^{3} \)
Simplify inside the parenthesis first:
\( \frac{2a^{2}b^{2}}{b^{-1}a^{4}} = 2(a^{2-4})(b^{2-(-1)}) \)
\( = 2a^{-2}b^{3} \)
Now apply the outside power of 3:
\( (2a^{-2}b^{3})^{3} \)
Distribute the power of 3:
\( 2^{3}(a^{-2})^{3}(b^{3})^{3} \)
\( = 8a^{-6}b^{9} \)
Write with positive exponents:
\( = \frac{8b^{9}}{a^{6}} \)
Example 2.3
Simplify the following expression:
\( (((\sqrt{3})^{3})^{-2})^{-1} \)
Apply the Power of a Power rule repeatedly:
Multiply the exponents: \( 3 \times (-2) \times (-1) \)
\( = 6 \)
Apply the new exponent to the base:
\( (\sqrt{3})^{6} \)
Since \( (\sqrt{3})^{2} = 3 \):
\( (\sqrt{3})^{6} = ((\sqrt{3})^{2})^{3} = 3^{3} \)
\( = 27 \)

🌟 Idea 3: Advanced Simplification & Proofs

Factoring Bases & Proofs

Tap to reveal core secrets 👆

Key Strategies

  • Prime Factorization: Always convert composite bases (like 9, 18, 36) into prime numbers (2, 3, 5, 7) before applying power rules.
  • Fractional Bases: Remember that \( \sqrt{a} = a^{\frac{1}{2}} \), so \( (\sqrt{a})^{2x} = a^{x} \).
  • Substitution: In "Find the value" questions, simplify completely first, then plug in the value of the variable.
Example 3.1
Simplify:
\( \frac{4^{x+2} \times 9^{3+x}}{6^{2x+3}} \)
Convert bases to prime numbers:
\( 4 = 2^{2} \Rightarrow 4^{x+2} = (2^{2})^{x+2} = 2^{2x+4} \)
\( 9 = 3^{2} \Rightarrow 9^{3+x} = (3^{2})^{3+x} = 3^{6+2x} \)
\( 6 = 2 \times 3 \Rightarrow 6^{2x+3} = 2^{2x+3} \times 3^{2x+3} \)
Substitute back:
\( \frac{2^{2x+4} \times 3^{6+2x}}{2^{2x+3} \times 3^{2x+3}} \)
Subtract exponents for base 2: \( 2^{(2x+4)-(2x+3)} = 2^{1} = 2 \)
Subtract exponents for base 3: \( 3^{(6+2x)-(2x+3)} = 3^{3} = 27 \)
Multiply results: \( 2 \times 27 \)
\( = 54 \)
Example 3.2
Simplify, then find the value of the expression when \( n = 2 \):
\( \frac{9^{2n-1} \times 18^{1-4n}}{36^{2-3n}} \)
Convert all bases to primes (2 and 3):
\( 9^{2n-1} = 3^{4n-2} \)
\( 18^{1-4n} = 2^{1-4n} \times 3^{2-8n} \)
\( 36^{2-3n} = 2^{4-6n} \times 3^{4-6n} \)
Group similar bases and simplify:
Base 2: \( 2^{(1-4n)-(4-6n)} = 2^{2n-3} \)
Base 3: \( 3^{(4n-2+2-8n)-(4-6n)} = 3^{2n-4} \)
Simplified form: \( 2^{2n-3} \times 3^{2n-4} \)
Substitute \( n = 2 \):
\( 2^{4-3} \times 3^{4-4} = 2^{1} \times 3^{0} = 2 \times 1 \)
\( = 2 \)
Example 3.3
Simplify, then find the value of the expression when \( x = 1 \):
\( \frac{4^{x} \times 49^{x-1}}{98^{x}} \)
Convert bases to primes:
\( 4^{x} = 2^{2x} \)
\( 49^{x-1} = 7^{2x-2} \)
\( 98^{x} = 2^{x} \times 7^{2x} \)
Simplify by dividing:
Base 2: \( 2^{2x-x} = 2^{x} \)
Base 7: \( 7^{2x-2-2x} = 7^{-2} = \frac{1}{49} \)
Simplified form: \( \frac{2^{x}}{49} \)
Substitute \( x = 1 \):
\( = \frac{2}{49} \)
Example 3.4
Prove that:
\( \frac{27^{x-1} \times 8^{x}}{(2\sqrt{2})^{2x} \times (3\sqrt{3})^{2x}} = \frac{1}{27} \)
Numerator: \( 3^{3x-3} \times 2^{3x} \)
Denominator: \( (2 \times 2^{0.5})^{2x} = 2^{3x} \) and \( (3 \times 3^{0.5})^{2x} = 3^{3x} \)
Cancel out \( 2^{3x} \) from top and bottom:
\( \frac{3^{3x-3}}{3^{3x}} = 3^{-3} = \frac{1}{27} = \text{RHS} \) (Proved!)
✍️ Try It Yourself 3
If \( 3^{a} = 2 \), find the value of:
\( 9^{a} + 27^{a} \)
Express bases in terms of \( 3^{a} \):
\( 9^{a} = (3^{2})^{a} = (3^{a})^{2} = (2)^{2} = 4 \)
\( 27^{a} = (3^{3})^{a} = (3^{a})^{3} = (2)^{3} = 8 \)
Add results: \( 4 + 8 \)
\( = 12 \)

🌟 Idea 4: Applications & Equations

Geometric & Exponential Equations

Tap to reveal core secrets 👆

Key Concepts

  • Tiling Area: \( \text{Number of tiles} = \frac{\text{Area of room}}{\text{Area of one tile}} \)
  • Exponential Equations: If \( a^{f(x)} = a^{g(x)} \), then \( f(x) = g(x) \).
Example 4.1
If \( 5^{x+1} = 10^{x} \), find the value of \( 4^{x} \).
Rewrite \( 10^{x} \) as \( (5 \times 2)^{x} = 5^{x} \times 2^{x} \):
\( 5^{x} \times 5^{1} = 5^{x} \times 2^{x} \)
Divide both sides by \( 5^{x} \):
\( 5^{1} = 2^{x} \Rightarrow 2^{x} = 5 \)
We need \( 4^{x} = (2^{2})^{x} = (2^{x})^{2} \):
\( (5)^{2} = 25 \)
\( = 25 \)
✍️ Try It Yourself 4.1
A square room has a side length of \( 2x^{3}y^{2} \) units. The room is to be completely tiled using square tiles, each with side length \( xy^{2} \) units. How many tiles are required to cover the entire floor?
Area of room = \( (2x^{3}y^{2})^{2} = 4x^{6}y^{4} \)
Area of one tile = \( (xy^{2})^{2} = x^{2}y^{4} \)
Number of tiles = \( \frac{4x^{6}y^{4}}{x^{2}y^{4}} \)
\( = 4x^{4} \text{ tiles} \)
✍️ Try It Yourself 4.2
Find the area of a square with side length \( 3a^{2}x^{3} \) cm.
Area of square = \( (\text{side})^{2} \)
\( = (3a^{2}x^{3})^{2} \)
Distribute power: \( 3^{2}(a^{2})^{2}(x^{3})^{2} \)
\( = 9a^{4}x^{6} \text{ cm}^{2} \)
